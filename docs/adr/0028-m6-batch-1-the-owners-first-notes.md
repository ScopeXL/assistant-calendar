# ADR 0028: The owner's first notes (M6, batch 1)

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-09

## Context

- Sunroom 0.6.0 went on the owner's wall and phones, and after the first day of use they sent
  fourteen notes. `docs/M6.md` turns them into tasks; the decisions below were made with the
  owner on 2026-10-09 and settled while building them (PLAN §15, M6 → 0.7.0).
- The rule that held until now was "sentence case everywhere" (ADR 0013, UX §7). The owner wants
  titles to read as titles ("Calendars & Accounts", "This Week").

## Decision

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| The case of words | Title Case for titles, headings and navigation: screen, page, room, sheet, panel and dialog titles, section headings (the Today panel's too), view and tab names, rail rooms and the header's quick jumps. Every word is capitalized except a, an, the, and, or, but, of, to, in, on, at, by, for, with, unless it comes first, last or after a colon. Everything else (buttons, toasts, hints, row labels, chips, body, errors) stays sentence case, and nothing is ever all caps. The design-rules test checks every title written as a literal (`title="…"`, an h1 to h3's text, the Settings pages). This supersedes ADR 0013's "sentence case everywhere" |
| "Kid" | The family reads **Child** and **children** everywhere: the role picker, the person rows, Child-safe editing, a child's phone, the hints. The stored value `kid` stays (`members.role`, `is_kid_device`, `kid_safe_editing`, `every_kid`, the error code `kiosk_not_kid`), so there's no migration and no API change |
| Birthdays on the calendar | A core overlay, `birthdays` (`calendar/birthdays.py` over the pure `domain/birthdays.py`), registered at app creation and always on, so a birthday shows whichever plugins are off. It reads the people through the household service, never a plugin. Each birthday is a real all-day chip in the person's color with a cake mark, and a tap opens a read-only sheet. Countdowns keeps counting birthdays down (Coming Up, the day's celebration) but stops drawing them on the calendar, so nothing shows twice |
| Live status | One component (`ui/LiveStatus.tsx`) merges the stream's status with whether the server answers into five states, told apart by glyph, never color alone: connected, reconnecting, checking every 30 seconds, offline, hidden once signed out. Offline wins. Anything but connected shows only after 2 seconds, kept outside React, because the wall reopens its stream on every room switch. It sits at the board's top-right corner and at the right of the phone Calendar's Show row (its header has no room beside the arrows) as a button whose tap says the state in words, and in About → Connection in place of the words |
| The two Week layouts | **Agenda** (the stacked chips, still the default) and **Hours** (a grid from midnight to midnight), the household's in Settings → Display → Week layout. A switch at the end of the board header's second line overrides it for this screen in the display state, and the zoom (24h, 12h, 1h, 15m) lives there too; both go back after two minutes idle, like the Today panel. Phones keep their views, and the Day view keeps its timeline |
| The zoom ladder and the grid | 24h fits the whole day under the sticky day heads, 12h half of it, 1h at least two full chips an hour, 15m at least four, each step at least double the one before (`features/calendar/hours.ts`, pure, in rem so Extra large scales). The grid is one CSS grid whose day columns are subgrids, so their heads and bodies line up; a column can't also be a size container (its layout containment would stop the subgrid), so the head and body are. Every browser Sunroom targets has subgrid; without it the columns would fall back to `display: contents` with fixed column starts |
| Tap targets in Hours | A chip's button is at least 3.5rem tall and its lane at least 3.5rem wide, so the 56 px rule holds; in one column a later chip sits on an earlier one's transparent tail, and the tap-target check allows exactly that overlap. A chip that would start less than 1.5rem below the chips before it joins their group side by side instead, so at least 24 px of every chip stays uncovered (WCAG 2.5.8; axe's target-size found it once the tips strip made the 24h grid shorter). A chip too small to read is what the zoom is for, the way a Month cell opens its day |
| Accounts from any device | Add an account, Connect again and Disconnect work on a phone, a computer and the wall screen alike, decided by the device's kind (`kiosk`), not the shell, so a laptop gets every way. On the wall, passwords are masked fields on its own keyboard and Settings is behind the PIN; the two Google ways that need a phone (the helper's key file, Google's own sign-in page) say "From a phone" and show a QR, and "Or add it from a phone" sits under the choices. Nothing on the wall opens a browser tab: Apple's page is "on a phone or computer". Back from Google's page, Calendars & Accounts reads `?google=…`, toasts how it went, opens a new account's calendars and cleans the address. ADR 0004 (the three Google ways) and ADR 0006 (secrets encrypted, never sent back) are unchanged; UX §11's "secrets are phone-only" is replaced |
| Photos from a computer | A computer adds photos itself: **Add photos** in the Photos room's header and **Add a photo** in Change a person, decided by the device's kind as accounts are. The kitchen screen keeps its code for a phone, since it has no files. The server already took photos from any signed-in device; the bare `/photos` now loads the app on a fresh browser (only the files under it are reserved), so the wall's code works on a phone that never opened Sunroom |
| An updated container | The stream's hello names the server's version (both hellos; the test server can stand one in). A page built from another version toasts "Sunroom was updated to 0.7.0. Refreshing…" and, 4 s later, unregisters its service worker, deletes every cache and reloads, keeping its address. It waits while an editor, a routine, a modal sheet or a save is open, checking every 5 s for 10 minutes at most, and reloads once per server version (`sessionStorage`), so a proxy still serving the old page can't loop it. The phone's pill and the wall's 3 AM reload stay as the fallback, and the wall's "reload" command reloads the same fresh way |
| Tips under the board | Settings → Display → Show tips (`show_tips`, off by default) puts a 56 px strip at the bottom of the wall's shell and a laptop's, never a phone's: one tip from a fixed list in the family's words (`features/tips/tips.ts`), a new one every half hour and on Next tip, never the same twice in a row, and only about plugins that are on. It's the last child of the shell's main column, so the board shrinks to make room and the Hours grid measures what's left; while it shows, `:root:has([data-tips])` sets `--tips-h` and the wall's toasts rise by it. It never says what's wrong or unused |
| The keyboard in a sheet | A modal sheet makes the page behind it inert, so the wall's keyboard couldn't type into a sheet until now. The keyboard mounts inside an open sheet's `<dialog>` while its field is focused, and the sheet sits above it (`bottom: var(--osk-h)`); a sheet that closes takes the keys with it. A tap on the backdrop closes a sheet only when the press started there, because the sheet moving up under a pressed field turns that click's target into the dialog |

## Consequences

- `household` gains `display_week_layout` (`agenda`/`hours`, default `agenda`) and `show_tips`
  (default off): one migration for the batch, `202610091752_household_week_layout_and_tips`.
- A page's name keeps its Title Case inside a sentence ("Settings → Calendars & Accounts"), and
  so do the plugins' names from their manifests ("Synced Calendars", "Photos & Screensaver").
- The rule is mechanical, so a small word stays lowercase even where it belongs to the verb
  ("Bring in Your Calendars", "Sign in with Google").
