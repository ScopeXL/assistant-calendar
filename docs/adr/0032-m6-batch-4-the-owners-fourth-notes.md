# ADR 0032: The owner's fourth notes (M6, batch 4)

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-14

## Context

- Sunroom 0.9.1 went on the owner's wall and phones, and the same day they sent a fourth set of
  notes: polish (the header, the theme, toasts, empty rooms, words), three questions (Coming Up,
  the update check, the end-to-end job on GitHub), the setup wizard, the wall's keyboard, and the
  time a batch takes to build. `docs/M6-batch-4.md` turns them into tasks, answers the questions
  and records every decision; this ADR keeps the ones that change an earlier decision or that a
  later session would otherwise have to rediscover (PLAN §15, M6 → 0.10.0).

## Decision

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| Verifying a batch | Two tiers, the owner's choice: each task passes the quick gate (`just quick`, its unit tests by path with `just test-fe` and `just test-py`, its one end-to-end spec on one project with `just e2e <spec> --project=<name>`), and the full gate (`just check`, the whole `just e2e`, the screenshots) runs once at the end of a batch and before any release. The recipes are terse, take arguments, and are on the command allowlist. Batch 3 ran the full gate after each of ten tasks and spent most of its two hours there. A failure found at the end is traced by running the failing spec at each task's commit; nothing is pushed before a release, and `just preflight` runs the full gate again |
| The fixture database | `just preflight` refuses a release whose newest migration has no `backend/tests/fixtures/db/<revision>.sql`. 0.9.0 shipped without its fixture, though `docs/RELEASING.md` said to build it, and the next migration's upgrade test would have failed; the 0.9.0 fixture was built before this batch's first task |
| Button case | Every button of up to five words is Title Case, a toast's action and a link that looks like a button included (Add Event, Save Changes, Check Now, Take It Back, Download Backup), the owner's choice; six words or more reads as a sentence and stays sentence case ("Type the household password here instead"). This narrows ADR 0028's rule, which kept action buttons in sentence case. Toasts, hints, labels, chips, segmented options, menu items, the choices in a list, empty-state copy and errors stay sentence case, and so does an icon-only button's screen-reader name. "from" joins the small words that stay lowercase ("Hide from Screensaver", "Code from another device" as a label), and the "in" of signing in may keep its capital ("Sign In Again"), while Google's Sign in with Google is written Google's way. The design-rules test reads every `<Button>`'s and header link's words, an `action` or `submit` prop's, and a toast's action through the TypeScript parser, each way a conditional or a template can come out, and fails on a label of two to five words that isn't Title Case; words a function returns (a sign-out label, a scope choice) are cased by hand |
| The zoom's order | **Fit the day · Zoom out · Zoom in**, left to right: the owner reads the whole day as the first of the three (their "Full"). Names, icons and when each is grey are unchanged; this changes ADR 0030's order |
| "Nothing on" | "Nothing today." and "Nothing else today." on the Today panel, the Today view and a phone's Today; "Nothing planned yet. Tap a time to add." on the Day view and "Nothing planned" in a phone's day list. The People view says a clear day with a **Free day** pill under the name, in the person's tint with the event chips' ink (neutral for Everyone; the person's own color as text on their dark-theme tint is 3.4:1, under AA): a positive word, and a pill because it's a state, not an error. It stays when the person's tasks follow. "Nothing on Groceries yet" is another sense and stays |
| The header's today block | The date, the time and the weather are each centred on the header's middle line (`items-center`); AM or PM stays on the time's own baseline. ADR 0030 put the date on the time's baseline, and against the centred 56 px weather button the time rode about 7 px high of the weather and the date low. The layout test measures the three middles within 2 px |
| The theme's first paint | Nothing put the theme on before the first paint: the shell applied the household's only once `/api/settings` answered, the page painted the browser's own light or dark first (or, on `/display`, the pair screen's guess: Auto by the device clock), and the fades were armed two frames after the shell mounted, usually before the settings came. So Light arriving on a page that painted dark faded in: the header, the rail and the Today panel over 2 s, today's column over 600 ms, buttons over 90 ms. Now the shells keep what they showed (theme, tint, text size, reduce motion, with the household's choices) in `localStorage["sunroom.appearance"]`; `public/theme-boot.js`, a classic same-origin script in `<head>` (the CSP runs no inline script), puts it on `<html>` before anything paints; the fades are armed only after the first appearance from settings; and the pair screen keeps a memory under five minutes old exactly (a refresh, the Pi's nightly restart), follows the household's remembered choices by its clock when it's older, and guesses only when there's none. A brand-new Pi still guesses |
| A theme change | Every color on the page crossfades together over the 600 ms UX §9 gives a theme switch: `lib/theme.ts` sets `data-theme-switching` on `<html>` for 600 ms when the theme changes once the fades are armed (never under Reduce motion), and one rule in `styles/motion.css` gives every element and pseudo-element a 600 ms transition on background, text, border, outline, decoration, fill, stroke and shadow while it's there. The 2 s daylight ease stays, on what draws the wall (`body` and the shells' roots), for the tint's steps. The surfaces' own crossfade rule went, and with it the rail's need to out-rank it: the rail's rule eases its width only. Dark looked like it had no animation because the owner's screens already painted dark |
| Toasts and sheets | **A toast stays where it was raised.** Each toast records the anchor that was newest when it was raised (`lib/toast`), and `ToastRegion` draws one group per anchor still on screen, the rest in the fixed region: one raised on the page stays on the page when a sheet opens over it (under the backdrop, timing out as usual), one raised in a sheet shows in it, and it goes back to the page when the sheet closes. Inside a sheet the anchor floats over the bottom of the body, above the footer, out of the layout, so the sheet never resizes for a toast; before, every toast was portaled into the newest anchor, jumped into a sheet that opened over it, and took its height plus 12 px in the sheet's flow, which `popLayout` gave back twice as it left. A sheet whose primary button is pinned in its body (`ui/PanelActions`) floats its toasts just above that bar instead (`ui/sheetToasts`), so they never cover it: the plan missed those sheets |
| Coming Up | Every countdown the family added always shows, however far out (someone chose to count down to it); a birthday only within **Birthdays in Coming Up** (Features → Countdowns): 1 week · 2 weeks · 1 month · 3 months · Any time, 1 month by default. A plugin setting (`birthdays_within`), so no migration; still three lines (one in the portrait band). The window is a `coming_up` flag on `GET /api/countdowns/upcoming`, which only the Today panel's block sends: the Countdowns room lists from the same route and keeps every birthday. The owner chose this over a per-countdown "show it anyway" switch and over one window for both kinds |
| Devices | The page is **Devices** (it was Phones & Screens). Two groups: **Paired Screens** on top, then **Phones & Computers**. Each row's icon says what it is (a screen, a phone, a tablet, a computer), from a `form` the server derives (`auth/password.device_form`) from the device's kind and the label `device_label` wrote at sign-in, which only a screen ever changes: no migration. The current device's button says what it is (Sign Out of This Phone, Tablet or Computer); **Child's Device**; **Add a Device** shows the one-time code, the QR, and its address for a computer; the sign-in screen's **Use a code from another device**. The end-to-end fixture's second phone sends a phone's user agent, as the phone projects do: it was a desktop browser at phone size, which the server now rightly calls a computer |
| Empty rooms | `ui/EmptyState` is centred: an icon in a soft circle (`bg-lit`, 112 px with a 56 px icon on the wall, 80 and 40 on phones), a short bold line ("No lists yet"), a note (the old sentence), and one large primary button. On the wall it fills the room and sits in its middle; on a phone it starts a third of the way down. Every room that uses it got an icon: Lists, Tasks, Photos, Countdowns, Saved Meals, Stars & Rewards. A phone screen's bottom bar already holds the room's button, so the phone's empty rooms show none of their own (a second "New List" a few hundred pixels above the bar's was clutter); Stars & Rewards, which has no bar, keeps its button. The kitchen screen's Photos puts its QR in the button's place. What's empty without being a room keeps a quiet line (`QuietLine`): a list with nothing on it, Meals' week with nothing planned (the week's grid is still there), Settings → Tasks' Rewards and Routines groups |
| The update check | It reads the repository's **tags** (`/repos/…/tags?per_page=100`) and takes the highest version among them, compared as numbers (0.10.0 is above 0.9.1); a tag that isn't a version is skipped. It asked `/releases/latest`, but the repository has every tag and no GitHub Release (the deploy skill tags and pushes, and never publishes one), so GitHub answered 404 and About said "GitHub's answer wasn't one Sunroom could read". A 404 or another error now says "GitHub couldn't find Sunroom's versions. It will try again tomorrow."; an answer it can't read keeps the old line. Publishing a Release per tag is a follow-up, and the check doesn't depend on it |
| An account's calendars | **Synced first, then by name**, in the sheet's Sync and Show on sections, ordered once as the sheet opens and kept while it's open, so a row never moves under a finger that just switched it on; opened again, it's ordered again. They came as the server lists them, by creation, which for a new account is discovery order |
| Apple | The chooser reads **Apple · Google · Holidays · Another calendar · A calendar server**, Apple's line "Your iPhone's and Mac's calendars, both ways." The account, its toasts ("Connected Apple · 3 calendars"), its sheet, the source line on events ("From Apple · Work") and the CalDAV error copy ("Apple said that password isn't right…", "That calendar isn't in your Apple account anymore.") all say **Apple**, and so does Apple's own newer word for the login, **Apple Account** (it was Apple ID). Accounts already named "iCloud" are renamed by a data migration (`202610141930`, forward-only): the word at the start of a CalDAV account's label, alone or before " · ", so the family never sees two names; a label someone typed otherwise keeps it. `docs/SYNC.md` keeps iCloud as the technical name of Apple's CalDAV. appleid.apple.com still serves Apple's sign-in (checked 2026-10-14), so the steps keep it |
| Setup's layout | Modelled on Pair This Screen, which the owner pointed to. From 1024 px (`features/onboarding/SetupFrame`) a centred card: the brand, how long it takes and **the six steps listed at the left** (done, current, upcoming; not tappable: the steps' Back buttons walk it) beside **the step's title, lead and form at the right**, both vertically centred, with the wall's title and lead sizes, the buttons on one row (Back or Later at the left, the main one at the right), and a new step rising in (never the first). Welcome and You're Set are one centred column with the brand lockup and one big button. On a phone: one column with the brand row, "2 of 6" and a six-segment progress bar. The steps, their order and their fields are unchanged; the plugins' steps (Where's Home, Your Calendars) keep their own buttons |
| The keyboard | Stays Sunroom's own (the owner's choice; why it isn't the Pi's squeekboard is in docs/M6-batch-4.md, Answers). **A full-width dock** from the rail to the screen's edge (the whole width before pairing and in portrait), its keys centred in a 960 px block, the same 80 × 64 keys (88 × 72 portrait), **a number row** above the letters on the letters and password layouts (the 123 page keeps its symbols; the number pad is unchanged). It was a 960 px card under the side panel, bottom-right: the owner found it unfamiliar. The rail side no longer mirrors a dock; the dock spans whatever isn't rail. No rounded corners or shadow: a dock, not a card. `--osk-h` follows the taller keys by measurement, as before |
| The e2e job | Playwright's base test timeout is 60 s; the two accessibility journeys ("a phone, signed in" and "the wall screen, from first light to Settings", about forty checks each) are `test.slow()`; the `guard` fixture awaits its `addInitScript`, so a test that skips itself at once reports skipped, not "Test ended."; the job caches the browsers by Playwright's version and has `timeout-minutes: 45`; CI keeps `retries: 1`. **The deploy skill waits for the push's CI run and reports it**: green, or the failing jobs with the run's link and a proposed fix for a working session, never fixed silently or from the deploy. It had failed on every release since 0.5.0, unseen, because the release gate is local and nothing looked at the run |
| The default calendar | Asked for once the batch was built. **Settings → Calendars & Accounts opens with Default calendar**, a value row whose list is every calendar that can be written to: the household's own, then an account's that isn't read-only, named after its account ("Apple · Family", as an event's sheet names it; Add's Calendar row names them the same way). It saves as it's picked, and the "New events go here" switch left each calendar's Edit sheet, so there is one place to choose; the chosen calendar's row says "Default calendar". An account's calendar can be it (until now only a local one could), so what's added on the wall can go to the family's own calendars. While the chosen one is unmapped with its account or turned read-only, new events go to the first local calendar, and it's the default again when it's back. **Sunroom always keeps one calendar of its own** (the owner's answer, once every one of them could go): the last one has no Remove, a line in its sheet says why, and the server refuses it, so new events always have somewhere to go |

## Consequences

- New recipes: `just quick`, `just test-fe`, `just test-py`, and `just e2e` with arguments;
  Playwright's local reporter is `line`. CLAUDE.md's definition of done and PLAN §14.7 are the
  two tiers.
- The design-rules test imports `typescript` to read the sources' JSX.
- One migration, data only: `202610141930_sync_accounts_named_icloud_become_apple`, tested
  against the 0.9.0 fixture database (built before the batch's first task), with its own 0.10.0
  fixture (`202610141930.sql`) so the next migration has one to test against.
- New in the frontend: `public/theme-boot.js` (a classic script in `<head>`, linted as a
  browser script) and `localStorage["sunroom.appearance"]`; `data-theme-switching` on `<html>`;
  `ui/sheetToasts`; `EmptyState`'s icon, title, note and action, and `QuietLine`;
  `features/onboarding/SetupFrame` with `StepActions` and `SetupEnd`; `KeyboardHost`'s `rail`.
  Gone: the surfaces' `data-surface` marks and their crossfade rule, the wizard's `Page`,
  `AddPhoneSheet` (now `AddDeviceSheet`).
- New in the API: `DeviceOut.form`; `GET /api/countdowns/upcoming?coming_up=true`; the
  countdowns plugin's `birthdays_within` setting (no version bump: a new key reads as its
  default).
- The end-to-end fixture's extra phone sends a phone's user agent (the Pixel 7's).
- The default calendar: `POST /api/calendar/calendars/{id}/default` (a parent's) replaces
  `PATCH …/calendars/{id} {is_default}`; `household.default_calendar_id` may name a synced
  calendar (no migration: the column was already there). `DELETE …/calendars/{id}` refuses the
  household's last local calendar (409 `last_calendar`). Pruning a synced calendar clears that
  link first, since the foreign key would stop it. `ValueRow`, `ChoiceRow` and
  `useFieldLabel` take `strong`, a Settings row's label, as `TextField` did.
- The deploy skill waits for the push's CI run and reports it; preflight needs the newest
  migration's fixture database.
- What wasn't built, and why, is in each task's "As built" note; the follow-ups are at the end
  of `docs/M6-batch-4.md`.
